# Praxis / A pin with a source

28 seconds · 1920×1080 · 30 fps · smooth clock · crisp geometry · silent.

## PAGE

Problem: an assistant can write plausible firmware while lacking the board facts that make it work.
Audience: embedded developers and AI engineers encountering the experimental Praxis alpha.
Goal: make them inspect the repository and try its source-backed MCP workflow.
Emotion: technical skepticism becomes curiosity through one concrete, traceable pin.

Assumption: a landscape promotional film for a GitHub/social launch. The current project is experimental; no universal MCU claim or newly performed flash is implied.

## Three different ideas

1. Transformation chain: manuals assemble into a processor. Rejected: generic ingestion metaphor, little actual proof.
2. Output montage: compiler and physical trials cut rapidly. Rejected: too many cases to read and risks implying broader qualification.
3. One-task documentary, staged as a moving technical atlas: follow LED_A through real responses, dependencies and the authored firmware. Selected: the exact PD12/R40/LD4 case and the stone octopus make this Praxis rather than a generic AI tool.

Cover/swap test: remove the name and this still contains Praxis's actual MCP tool contracts, MB997 pin mapping, dependency route and original material. A competing chatbot cannot inherit these demonstrations by changing a logo.

## World and time

All acts share the user's dark stone artwork, directional cool light, warm graphite document planes and restrained cyan selection. Depth comes from projected paper sheets, parallax and one custom Three.js silicon-package illustration. No stock images, generated assets, fabricated UI or audio. The documentation screenshot is captured from the existing independent site, without modifying it.

Hero moment: the LED_A response opens into its complete two-net circuit: PD12 on one side of R40, LD4_ANODE on the other. A cyan signal crosses the component intentionally without merging the two electrical nets. This is a visualization of existing evidence, not a live electrical measurement.
